# m3d3

Godot 4.7 project, N64 (Ocarina of Time / Majora's Mask) inspired.

Run the game (F5) to start in the overworld; walk through the stone doorway to enter the temple.

## N64 Level Tools (`addons/n64_level_tools`)

Mesh-based brushes (own vertex/polygon data → `ArrayMesh`, no CSG), stored as data, not nodes.

- **LevelRoot** (node) — the only node in the scene. Displays and bakes a `LevelData`.
- **LevelData** (resource) — all brushes + the baked mesh/collision. Inline in the scene or its own `.res`/`.tres` ("Save Data As…" in the panel). External files are written on scene save.
- **LevelBrush** (sub-resource) — shape params, transform, material, bake mode, vertices + faces. Selecting a brush shows it in the inspector.

### Floating panel (top-left of the 3D viewport; drag title to move)

- **Add shape** — Block, Wall, Plane, Grid, Ramp, Stairs, Cylinder, Pyramid. Click shape, click viewport. Snaps to grid, stacks on brush faces. Shift-click keeps placing, Esc cancels.
- **Object mode** — click brushes (Shift adds). **Sticky grid** (toolbar grid button or **G**; **Shift+G** re-takes the current snap and the height under the cursor; **]** / **[** or Alt+PgUp / PgDn raise / lower it one locked step, to see it on top of raised objects; the status line shows its height): a grid at the snap size when turned on — changing the snap afterwards leaves it as is — (stronger lines every metre) across the whole level on a horizontal plane at the height of the surface under the cursor when turned on — stays put whatever the cursor is over, buildings hide it (depth-tested); for laying out greyboxes. Editor only, never saved. Hovering shows a small snap-size reference grid (yellow, fading out 5 cells away) on the surface under the cursor, on world snap lines — where snapped moves and resizes land, whatever the brush's own cell size (the white hover outline is the brush's real edges). Arrows move on X/Y/Z, centre square on XZ. Coloured squares on the brush sides resize it. **H** hides the selected brushes (Paint mode: the brush under the cursor; Vertex/Edge/Face: the hovered one), **Alt+H** shows them all — editor-only, not saved: hidden brushes aren't drawn, picked or painted, so you can paint what's behind or under them; the bake and the game ignore it (the status line counts them). Rot ±90 (**R** / **Shift+R**), Dup (Ctrl+D), Del (Delete). While placing a brush, **R** / Shift+R turns the ghost 90° first (ramps and stairs show an uphill arrow); the turn sticks for the next placements.
- **Vertex / Edge / Face modes** — click or box-drag to select (Shift adds; **Cmd/Ctrl+click** selects the shortest path from the last picked vertex / edge / face, like Blender). Drag gizmo arrows to move on X/Y/Z (centre square: XZ), snapped. **Ctrl+drag** an arrow (extrudes along that arrow, either way) or press **E** (one snap step along the surface) to extrude. **Shift+E** extrudes the selected border outwards instead, each edge along its own direction in the surface, corners diagonally: a floor grows on every side. Extrude: faces; open edges (grow floors); or, in Vertex mode, selected vertices along an open border — select a floor's border vertices and extrude up to raise walls (the floor stays). **F** / *Make face* fills a face through the selected vertices or edges (Blender's F): a loop of open edges, like the top of extruded walls, is capped with consistent winding; other selections are ordered around their centre and face the camera. New faces take a neighbour's texture. Walls/caps grown from a floor face inward (a room seen from inside); Flip faces for an exterior. **R** / *Ramp*: an even slope between two rows — raise the top row to its height, select the foot row, the top row and every vertex between, press R (Face mode works too: select the faces from the top down to the foot). The highest vertices stay; the low row farthest from them is the foot and stays; everything between gets the height for how far it sits from the foot toward the top (measured flat), even if it starts flat on the ground. Select a strip about as wide as the top: vertices beside the top's edge are levelled to its height. Extrude / Flip / Delete / **Subdivide** / **Move…** buttons. Click another brush to switch.
- **Nudge** (any mode with a selection, incl. whole brushes in Object mode): arrow keys move one snap step along the ground relative to the camera (Up = away from you), PageUp / PageDown along Y, Shift ×10. **Move…** applies an exact X/Y/Z offset.
- **Subdivide** (Face mode): splits selected faces into quads through edge midpoints and a centre vertex (quad → 4); neighbours get the midpoints too, so no cracks. Base texture / UV tweaks carry over, painted masks are cropped to each child, new vertices average their neighbours' vertex colours. Re-bake lighting afterwards. Use it wherever lighting or vertex paint needs more vertices.
- **Snap** — grid size in metres, also drives the debug checker. "Snap Sel" snaps vertices or brush origins.
- **Debug grid texture** — toggle snap-sized checker vs real materials.
- **Bake Level** — non-destructive, undoable. Culls hidden faces between touching brushes, merges coplanar faces within a brush, drops degenerate tris, welds verts, one surface per material, trimesh collision. Stairs bake a smooth collision ramp by default. "Preview baked" shows the result.

- **Play here** (▶ in the bake row): bakes every level in the open zone (re-lighting baked modes), saves the scene and its level data, and runs the zone with the player standing on the reference character (facing its way) if shown, else on the surface at the centre of the 3D view (facing the way the camera looks), else at the default spawn. Starts at the Paint panel's *Preview hour*; picks the Room under that spot. Editor runs only: the hand-off (`addons/n64_level_tools/play_here.gd`, one-shot `user://play_here.json`) never ships.
- **Reference character** (person icon, "R", in the view row of the toolbar): an editor-only, player-sized capsule placed on the surface at the centre of the view. It shows height, eye / camera height, facing, and jump reach (feet and head at the top of a jump). **Alt+click** a surface to move it. Sizes come from `player/player.gd` (`BODY_RADIUS`, `BODY_HEIGHT`, `CAMERA_HEIGHT`, `JUMP_VELOCITY`) and project gravity, so it tracks the real player. Never saved into the scene or shipped.

- **Grid resize** (Object mode; toolbar *Grid resize…*, or **Alt+drag** a resize square): grows or shrinks a brush along X / Y / Z without stretching its cells. The border on that side moves (walls there move with it), and new edge loops are cut at the border's cell spacing, so a 1 m floor grid stays 1 m and side walls gain matching columns; dragging snaps to whole cells. Shrinking removes cells past the new border. Painted masks stay on their cells (new cells start unpainted), vertex colours carry over. The dialog sets axis, side (+ / − / both) and exact metres. A plain drag on a square still scales proportionally. Re-bake lighting afterwards.

### Modifying shapes

1. Parametric: inspector `shape`, `size`, `segments`, or drag resize squares in Object mode — brush regenerates.
2. Freeform: Vertex/Edge/Face modes. After a hand edit the brush stops regenerating from params; resize squares then scale its vertices. "Rebuild From Shape" (inspector) resets to the parametric shape.

At runtime only the bake is used (unbaked levels auto-bake at load with a warning).


## N64 textures (bottom panel "N64 Textures")

Import hi-res images (1024+) as N64-spec `N64Texture` resources (an `ImageTexture`, usable in any material).

- **Add Images…** from anywhere on disk. Names are parsed for category (grass, ground, rock, cement, tiles, brick, wood, roof, metal, water, fabric, plants, decal, detail, misc), usage, tiling and format. PBR non-colour maps (normal, roughness, AO, height, metalness, opacity…) are skipped. Resolution/map noise is stripped from names (`Bricks076_1K-JPG_Color` → `bricks076`).
- **Tiling**: Seamless (both axes), Seamless U / V (planks, bricks), None (decals/details: resized only, keeping aspect).
- **Zoom**: tiling textures use a 1/zoom subsection of the photo (closer-up, chunkier N64 detail), made seamless with an image-quilting seam (Efros & Freeman): the edge band overlaps the real pixels past the opposite edge and joins them along a minimum-error cut (2 px feather), so there's no cross-fade ghosting. **Auto zoom** (default for surfaces) measures detail size and repeating patterns (autocorrelation) so features land ~2.5 texels and pattern repeats ~12 texels (≥ 2 per tile), and places the region on the most typical-looking patch, avoiding outlier specks. One-direction textures (bricks, planks) measure their course spacing from row / column averages and crop a whole number of courses — *Courses* per repeat (0 = auto, ~16 texels per course; fewer = bigger bricks), even unless Row shuffle is on — with the repeat edge on a mortar line. They import as CI4 64×64 (the largest colour-indexed size that fits TMEM) with **Row shuffle** anti-tiling: each row of repeats slides sideways by a random amount, so repeats stop stacking into columns while courses stay whole. Moving the slider switches to manual; click/drag the Source preview to move the region.
- **Formats** (TMEM-budgeted sizes): RGBA32, RGBA16 (5551), IA16/IA8/IA4, I8/I4 (greyscale, tint with vertex/prim colour), CI8 (256-colour palette), CI4 (16). Palettes: median cut + k-means, stored as RGBA5551; transparent texels get a reserved entry.
- **Watch folder**: tick *Watch* (defaults to ~/Downloads) and download textures in your browser as usual — logged-in sites like sketchuptextureclub work because the browser does the download. New images and archives (zip, rar, 7z) are picked up once the download finishes (`.crdownload` partials are ignored); archives are unpacked (rar / 7z through the system `bsdtar`, built into macOS) and only colour maps kept. *Auto-import* skips the review queue. Names containing `seamless` / `tileable` are treated as already tiling (no seam blending, tiled both ways).
- **Tile size** (World size, metres per repeat) defaults to **Auto**: width in texels ÷ the project **texel density** (Project Settings → `n64/textures/texels_per_metre`, default 32), so texels are the same size on every surface — 64 px → 2 m, 32 px → 1 m. Change the density to rescale everything at once; set a number on a texture (or per level via the Paint panel's *Tile size*) to override.
- Previews: source, N64 result, 3×3 tiling, palette. Settings apply to all selected items.
- Output: `res://textures/<category>/<name>.res`; the hi-res original is copied to `res://textures/_source/` (ignored by Godot). Each texture keeps its settings — change them in the inspector and press **Rebuild From Source**.

## Painting (Paint mode — brush icon in the toolbar)

- **Variation** (Base: per texture to apply, per level; overlay layers: per layer): breaks up repetition by brightening / darkening the texture with one shared seamless noise (`addons/n64_runtime/textures/variation_noise.res`). *Variation* = strength (0 = off), *Variation size* = metres per noise repeat (bigger = broader patches), *Variation mode* = Light & dark or Darken only. Sampled in each face's world-aligned projection and turned ~37° against the texture grid, so walls and floors vary independently and the noise never lines up with the texture's repeat. One extra texture sample per textured layer.
- **Soft Fill by Normal** smooths normals only across faces within 50° (like the baked lighting), so filling walls never bleeds onto a floor they were extruded from (and vice versa); gentle slopes still blend softly.
- **Shade layers**: an overlay with no texture paints a flat *Colour* (default black) through its mask — with a noise mask that's darkening spots, painted it's grime / fake shadow. *Opacity* sets the strength (~0.3–0.4 for subtle patches); any blend mode works.
- **Noise mask** (overlay layers): the layer shows where the same shared noise is high — *Noise mask* = coverage (0 = off), *Noise size* (m), *Noise softness*, *Noise combine*: Noise only (ignores painting), Painted + noise, or Break up painting (painted × noise). *Noise only on* limits it to faces with one base texture, so e.g. a moss layer covers the brick walls in patches while a dirt layer breaks up the grass floor, each with its own settings. Uses a rotated, offset copy of the noise (independent of Variation) in each face's own projection, and works with every blend mode (Height gives ragged, texture-following edges; Mix gives soft ones).
- **Collapsible sections**: click a Paint panel heading (Layers, Tool, Brush, Level) to fold it away; remembered per project. Collapsed *Layers* still names the layer you're painting ("Layers · Dirt path").
- **Texture slots** (*Texture to apply*, overlay layer *Texture*): click to browse the imported library — `res://textures` grouped by category folder in FileSystem order, with thumbnails; type to filter by file name (every word must match), Enter picks the first match, ↓ moves into the list. *None* clears; you can also drop a texture from the FileSystem dock.
- **Base layer**: every face has a base texture (palette in `LevelData.textures`). Brush / Fill Face / Fill by Normal assign the "Texture to apply"; Shift (or Erase) clears it. Unassigned faces use the brush's `material` if set, else neutral grey. *Tile size* sets metres per repeat for the texture to apply in this level (default: the texture's own World size; stored in `LevelData.base_tile_sizes`) and updates every face using it immediately — no refill needed.
- **Overlay layers** (up to 4, bottom → top): texture, blend (Mix, Multiply, Add, Subtract, Screen, Overlay, Height), opacity, tile size, visibility. Painted through per-face masks. **Height** blend is for terrain transitions (path into grass): instead of cross-fading along the brush falloff, each texel goes to whichever texture is "taller" (brighter than its own average), so mud fills the gaps between grass clumps. *Edge sharp* (soft → texel-crisp), *Edge noise* (world-space wander, gaps and islands) and *Edge darken* (damp / trampled band) shape it. The edge sits where the mask is about half covered, so paths read slightly narrower than with Mix.
- **Tools**: Brush (size, strength, hardness falloff, custom brush shapes from `res://brushes/` — "Add…" imports greyscale images), Erase (or hold Shift), Fill Face, Fill by Normal (Auto — the kind of face you click: floor, wall or ceiling — or facing up / down / walls / all, within an angle; on overlay layers **Soft edge (°)** blends coverage per texel from smoothed vertex normals across that angle band — 0 = whole faces). `[` `]` change brush size. Strokes behave like Photoshop opacity: overlapping stamps in one stroke never exceed the strength. Everything is undoable.
- **Vertex colour** (last entry in the layer list): per-vertex colour that multiplies the final shade, like the N64's vertex colours (white = unchanged). Brush / Fill Face / Fill by Normal (soft edge uses smoothed normals) with a colour picker; Shift erases back to white. Detail follows mesh density — add Grid segments where you want finer shading. Colours follow vertices through extrude / delete; faces with vertex colour aren't merged by the bake. **Bake vertex AO** (in that section): ray-traced ambient occlusion per vertex ("dirty vertex colours") — inside corners, wall bases and spots under overhangs darken. Stored in its own channel (`LevelBrush.vertex_ao`, rendered through the vertex colour alpha), multiplying your painted colours without changing them; *Clear* removes it, *Add to existing AO* stacks passes (e.g. tight + broad). *AO strength*, *AO radius* (small = tight corner/edge darkening, large = broad shading) are the level's AO settings (LevelData → Lighting, shared with the lighting bake); changing them — in the panel or the inspector — re-bakes existing vertex AO automatically. Toolbar **AO** toggle hides it in the editor (shader global `n64_vertex_ao`) to paint without it; the game always shows it. Works in every lighting mode (the Baked modes also have their own AO in the lighting bake). Detail follows vertex density. Undoable.
- **AO only (keep real-time lighting)**: toolbar **Bake AO** (next to the light bulb) bakes vertex AO with the Paint panel's AO settings and leaves the level in Dynamic lighting — corners and creases darken, scene lights stay real-time. Baked corner lighting only applies in Baked Vertex mode, so switching a level back to Dynamic ignores a stored lighting bake (switch back to Baked Vertex to use it again).
- **Vertex lighting** (toolbar light-bulb button, or automatically on Bake when a level's Lighting mode is *Baked Vertex*): N64-style per-corner lighting baked from the scene's DirectionalLight3D / OmniLight3D nodes (ray-traced shadows; lights with Bake Mode = Disabled are skipped), sky/ground ambient and ambient occlusion (settings in LevelData → Lighting). Corner normals smooth only across faces within 50°, so boxes keep hard edges. Stored separately from hand-painted vertex colours (they multiply), so re-lighting never loses painting. Baked-vertex levels render unlit (scene lights still light actors); the `n64_world_tint` global — driven by DayNightSun — tints them for time of day. Quality follows tessellation: subdivide floors (Grid brushes) where light pools should show.
- **Baked Timed lighting** (LevelData → Lighting mode *Baked Timed*): bakes the vertex lighting once per key of the project's **LightingSchedule** (up to 4 keys) and stores each in a mesh custom channel (CUSTOM0..3); the shader blends the two keys bracketing the current hour, so lighting changes smoothly with `GameState.time_of_day` at no runtime raytracing cost. The schedule is per game: **Project Settings → n64/lighting/schedule** (default `res://game/lighting_schedule.tres`). Each `LightingKey` has an hour, sun colour / energy (the sun's direction comes from the hour), sky / ground ambient and a *local lights* multiplier for OmniLights (lanterns on at night, off at noon). Blending wraps past midnight. Edit the hours/colours, then **Bake All** to re-light every timed level. DayNightSun follows the same keys, so actors match. Preview in the editor with Paint panel → **Preview hour**.
- **Clear paint** (Paint on, overlay layer selected): removes that layer's paint from the face-mask faces, or the whole level when nothing is masked; the layer and its settings stay. Undoable.
- **Reset faces** (Paint on, under the layer list): puts the face-mask faces back to plain base texture: removes paint on every overlay layer, overlay turns / border frames, vertex colour and surface overrides. Base texture and UV stay. Undoable.
- **Merge faces** (Paint on, under the layer list): joins face-mask faces that touch, lie in one plane and match (base texture, UV, overlay set, surface) into bigger faces; painted overlay masks are resampled onto the merged face. For leftover squares a border's corner cuts split; whole grid squares merge too, so mask only the pieces. Undoable. Border corner cuts are remembered per face (`LevelBrush.face_pieces`), so Shift-erasing a border merges its own pieces back by itself (other borders' cuts stay).
- **Fences**: a fence is one level brush (Object mode: select, move on the grid, duplicate, delete like any brush). The **Fence** button (Object mode) drops one on the ground where the view looks and selects it; in Vertex mode with a line of vertices selected it follows them. With the fence the only brush selected: **drag** a point to move it (x / z snap, it sits on the ground beneath), click a section's **+** to split it, **Shift+click** the ground to extend from the nearer end, **Alt+click** a point to remove it / a section to open or close it (open sections get the arch); the **resize squares** stretch it (its points scale; posts and panels fill the new length). Each pair of points is a section: any direction, its own slope. Inspector: **Fence Spacing** (distance between posts, exact; the last span of a section takes the rest; 0 = the style's), **Fence Follow Ground** (on: every post stands on the ground beneath it, sampled with a few rays around it so grid lines don't let it slip through — moving the fence onto a platform just works; off: posts on the points and the straight lines between them, for bridges / ledges), **Fence Sag** (metres the posts between the points sink at the middle of each section: a droopy, rickety look), the style. Posts reach *Post sink* (style) into the ground. Geometry is generated from these (LevelRoot via `fence_builder.gd`): flat, double-sided **cutout** quads (`LevelBrush.cutout`: the base texture's alpha cuts holes) with fitted textures (`LevelBrush.corner_uvs`) — a panel per span (whole repeats), posts as one quad along each section meeting them (straight posts: one; corners: one per side, so the panels end flush and no gap shows), wider end caps at the ends and beside openings (crossed when alone), an arch over each opening — plus collision-only wall faces in the same brush (`LevelBrush.face_bake`: never drawn): one slab per run of closed sections, *Collision thickness* thick, mitred at the posts so the player slides along it without catching, faces pointing out. Fences don't cast baked shadows. The look is a `FenceStyle` resource (default `res://game/world/fence_default_style.tres`): textures, panel height / lift, post size, end-cap width / height, arch height / clearance, default post spacing, slope mode, collision. **Generate fence textures** (inspector button on the style): pick a *Kind* (two rail, three rail, picket, lattice, boards), a rail material (wood) and optionally a post material (*Material grain*: Auto detects which way the wood runs; rails, arches and slats get grain along them, posts, pickets and boards up and down); it draws the panel, post, end cap and arch textures (alpha = gaps, materials sampled at their own scale, light / dark edges) and saves them as N64Textures next to the style (`<style>_panel.res`, …), updating them in place when generated again. The generator is tooling (level tools addon); only the textures ship.
- **Ropes**: a rope is one level brush hanging between its points (washing lines, lantern strings between buildings). The **Rope** button (Object mode) waits for two clicks: where it hangs from, then where it goes to; each anchor lands where the mouse meets the level and is pulled onto a **corner** of what it hits when one is near the mouse (green dot), so it ties to building corners. Selected alone, it edits like a fence: **drag** a point (same corner snapping), click a section's **+** to add a point where the rope hangs (an anchor in the middle), **Shift+click** to extend from the nearer end, **Alt+click** a point to remove it / a section to leave it out (two ropes in one brush); the resize squares stretch it (up / down too). Inspector: **Rope Sag** (metres each section hangs below the straight line at its middle; a new rope gets ~8% of its length) and the style. Geometry (LevelRoot via `rope_builder.gd`): each section a parabola cut into pieces about the style's *Segment length*, drawn as a cutout ribbon *Width* wide — two crossed (flat and upright) when *Crossed* is on, so it reads from below and the side — with the texture repeated along it in whole repeats (fitted `corner_uvs`, following the curve's length). Drawn only: no collision, no baked shadows. The look is a `RopeStyle` (`addons/n64_level_tools/rope_style.gd`; default `res://addons/n64_level_tools/rope/rope_default_style.tres` with a generated twisted-hemp texture `res://textures/rope/rope_default.res`); its texture runs left to right (the rope horizontal in the image), its height across the rope.
  - **Rope Hangs** (inspector list of `RopeHang`, `addons/n64_level_tools/rope_hang.gd`; several mix): *Kind* Scene (an instance at each spot: lanterns; generated nodes under the LevelRoot, shipped on export), Tassel (shimenawa straw tassel, crossed cards), Shide (zigzag paper streamer), Banner (cloth, *Cloth / Trim colour* and a *Crest*; its texture is generated per combination as `textures/rope/banner_<cloth>_<trim>_<crest>.res`) or Card (your own cutout texture). Cards are built into the rope's geometry (one draw, baked like the rope), hanging from the bottom of their cord (*Drop*, in the rope's texture) at *Size*, the texture fitted once, in the rope's plane (and across it when *Crossed*). *Placement*: Spaced (*Spacing*, evened out per section; *Offset* shifts by part of a gap, e.g. shide at 0.5 between tassels), Section middle, or Points (the rope's in-between anchors). Presets in `addons/n64_level_tools/rope/hangs/` (lantern, shimenawa_tassel, shide, shrine_banner, shop_banner); `rope/shimenawa_style.tres` is a thick straw rope. Ropes made before the list existed have their lantern settings moved into it on load.
  - **Banner text** (in-game script): a banner's *Text* is English (new line = new column / line), turned into sounds (ARPAbet, from `glyphs/cmudict/cmudict.dict` plus the project word list `glyphs/words.txt`, which wins: add names / invented words there) and drawn in its *Alphabet* in *Ink colour*; the inspector's *Text Sounds* shows each word's sounds and flags anything unknown or that wouldn't read back the same. Words that sound alike carry a spelling mark under the word (drawn in the alphabet, else a line with tally ticks = which one; the most common spelling, by `glyphs/frequency/en_50k.txt`, has none), so decoding gives the exact English back (lower case; . , ? ! hyphen and dash kept). Writing keeps one size; longer text makes a longer banner (64 x 128 / 192 / 256, the cloth longer to match). Alphabets: **SyllabaryAlphabet** (`addons/n64_level_tools/glyphs/syllabary_alphabet.gd`) — your own hand-drawn syllabary: *Make template* (inspector) writes a PNG drawing sheet (16 x 16 px glyphs at 4x, light-blue guides ignored, dark pixels = ink) with consonant base shapes, vowel marks, finals (consonants with no vowel after), punctuation, an optional 25 x 15 syllable grid (empty squares are built from base + mark) and optional spelling marks 1 - 15; the inspector previews sample text and lists what isn't drawn yet; saving the PNG rebuilds banners using it. `glyphs/syllabary/alphabet.tres` is the project's script and the default for banners (draw it in the **Script Builder** editor tab). **StrokeAlphabet** (`glyphs/pitman/alphabet.tres`, the earlier default) — Pitman-style joined outlines from editable stroke shapes. **GlyphAlphabet** — atlas blocks (the first draft). Only the drawn textures ship (`textures/rope/banner_<hash>.res`): the English lives in level data / hang resources, alphabets, the template and `glyphs/` are never exported.
  - **Script Builder** (editor tab beside 2D / 3D / Script; plugin `addons/n64_script_builder`, tooling only, excluded from exports): draw a SyllabaryAlphabet's glyphs pixel by pixel. Left: the alphabet (default `glyphs/syllabary/alphabet.tres`) and a palette per section (consonants, vowel marks, finals, punctuation, the 25 x 15 syllable grid — squares built automatically from base + mark show faintly). Middle: a 16 x 16 canvas (left drag draw, right drag erase, Shift+click a line from the last pixel; Clear, Flip, nudge, Copy / Paste) with a faint guide of what the glyph goes with (the consonant under a vowel mark or final — pick which — or a syllable's base + mark). Right: any English text as it'd be written on a banner, what isn't drawn yet, and each word's sounds. *Zones* (the alphabet's Zone Base / Mark / Final and Margin, a drawing guide only): outlines on the canvas (green base, orange vowel mark, purple final, red margin band), the current glyph kind's in bold, its pixels outside it drawn red; Alt+drag on the canvas sets the current kind's zone. Every stroke is one undo step and saves the alphabet (`glyphs`, stored in the resource; they win over its template PNG — *Copy squares drawn on the template* brings a painted template in), and banners using it rebuild.
- **Follow slope** (Paint on, base texture, under Rotation): the base texture lies on each painted face's own plane instead of being projected along the nearest axis, so ramps show it unstretched with rows level across the slope (overlays follow the face's UV too). Axis-aligned faces look identical either way; leave it off on bumpy terrain (each face gets its own seam). Stored per face as a negative UV scale; Pick from face (I) takes it along.
- **A: align to edge** (Paint mode, over a face): turns and shifts the face's base texture so its rows run along the face edge nearest the cursor, starting exactly on it, with the image's top pointing into the face (**Shift+A**: the other way). Any angle (kept in the face UV's rotation; scale stays). Overlays on the face line up with the same edge too (a border's own turn / slant is dropped). With the hovered face in the face mask, every masked face lines up on that same world edge. Undoable. Pick from face (I) then painting copies the alignment to other faces.
- **Overlay sets** (Paint on, top): overlay layers come in named sets, each up to 4 layers. Every face uses one (Default unless assigned), so a barrel can use wood + metal while the ground uses dirt + grass. The picker chooses the set the layer list shows and edits; overlay painting only reaches faces of that set. **+ Set** adds an empty set, the name field renames it, **Assign to masked** gives the face-mask faces the edited set (paint they have stays: slot 1 shows the new set's layer 1). The line under it shows which sets the masked faces use ("mixed" when several). Each extra set in use costs one more material / draw call per chunk; the shader is the same. Faces drawn with a brush `material` (no palette texture) always use Default. Icon buttons next to **+ Set**: duplicate (layers copied, paint not), move up / down, delete (its faces go back to Default and lose the overlay paint made for it). **Pick from face (I)** also switches to the face's set. Holding Cmd/Ctrl to mask shows the edited set's faces in orange (editing Default: faces of other sets in grey).
- **Border** tool (Paint): click a face to paint the selected overlay layer in a hard-edged band along the outer edge of the face mask (Cmd/Ctrl+click a face, Alt+Cmd+click a flat region, Cmd+drag a box — faces facing the camera with their centre inside, hidden ones included; Shift on release removes) — or, with no mask, of the flat surface clicked. Any selection shape, across brushes; *Width (m)* sets the band. E.g. a brick trim around a dirt square, trim along a house wall. Shift erases the band. *Texture follows the edge* (on by default) turns the overlay on each border face so the texture's width — a brick's long side — runs along the edge, rows running inward from it, and shifts it so the texture starts at the edge on every side (per face; diagonal borders snap to the nearer axis), so corners meet. The shifts live in `LevelData.overlay_offsets`, referenced per face from the face UV's rotation like the turn. *Sides*: the area's outline splits into sides at its corners (any shape: a ramp's side wall has its slope, base and back); the band runs on all of them by default — **Alt+click** an edge to leave that side out (dim red) or add it back; *All sides* resets (changing the face mask resets too). Sloped / diagonal edges: the texture also rotates freely to run exactly along them (`LevelData.overlay_angles`). *Soft edge (m)* fades the band's inner edge (with a Height blend it breaks up along the texture: grass creeping up a wall base); *Blend*, *Shadow* and *Edge darken* there are the selected layer's own settings, for a raised / darkened look. *Corners*: where the band turns between the texture's width and height, the faces there are cut (a few extra faces; base textures unchanged) so each side runs its own way — **Butt** (default): the band along the texture's width runs straight through the corner and the other stops against its inner edge; **Mitre**: the two meet on the 45° line. *Texture*: which way it lies on the band — **Along the edge** (default, a brick course), **Across the edge** (turned 90°: its width runs inward from the edge, e.g. soldier bricks or planks), or either **flipped** (upside down: a grass-edge fringe pointing the other way); rows stay lined up with the edge on every side. **Continue the neighbour (seamless)**: the band shows the layer's texture exactly as the face across the edge would if folded over it (its projection, scaled from its base tile to the layer's; a stair face with a stair-texture layer continues exactly as the stair draws itself: the stair's base stone folded over at its own tile, calmed and shaded by the step template in its own fold — `LevelData.overlay_stairs`), e.g. the stair texture wrapping onto a ramp's side, a hilltop's grass overlapping down its slope. Give the layer the neighbour's texture and Alt+click away the sides you don't want continued (each band face follows the one edge most of it is nearest). Stored as a general per-face overlay transform (`LevelData.overlay_bases`). Painting a border again replaces the old band at the new width, and Shift erases the whole band whatever the width box says (the width per border is kept in `LevelData.border_widths`). Blender set pieces aren't cut. **Turn overlay 90°** (Paint panel, any tool) turns the overlays — not the base — of the face-mask faces a quarter turn; stored per face in the face UV's rotation as whole extra turns, so it follows the face through extrude / delete / Blender and each turn gets its own material. Re-bake for the game.
- **Masks** live on each face in its world-aligned projection (`mask_density` px/m), so they stretch with vertex edits and follow faces through extrude/delete. They're packed into a per-level atlas for rendering and baking.
- **Shader**: world-aligned UVs in metres (+ per-face tweaks); non-square textures keep their aspect. Brush `material`s (StandardMaterial albedo texture/colour) are rendered through the same shader, so they get filtering and overlays too.
- **Mipmaps**: every N64Texture carries a Godot-generated mip chain (added on import / rebuild). The 3-point sampler picks the level from screen-space derivatives and blends between levels, so distant surfaces don't shimmer; nearest mode uses `filter_nearest_mipmap`.
- **Tint (colour combiner)** per texture: None, Multiply (texel × colour) or Gradient (`lerp(dark, light, texel)`, the N64 combiner `ENV + (PRIM − ENV) × TEXEL` on a greyscale texture). Organic categories (grass, ground, rock, cement, water) import as **CI4 + Gradient** with **Tint amount 0.5**: the palette is picked from the photo (average of its darkest / lightest ~15 %), the texel's luminance (within that range) indexes the gradient, and the amount mixes it with the texture's own colours (0 = original, 1 = full palette). Greyscale I4 + amount 1 gives the fully recolourable N64 intensity style. Palette overrides: per level for base textures (Paint panel → Base → *Level palette*, stored in `LevelData.base_tints`) and per overlay layer (*Palette → Override*). Tint is applied after filtering, so it works with 3-point, mipmaps and anti-tiling.
- **Anti-tiling** (per texture: Off / Offset / Offset + Rotate, hex cell size, *Anti Tiling Rotation*: how far each cell may turn, default 0.15 = ±27°; 1 = any angle, which reads as swirls on grass): hex tiling (Mikkelsen 2022, after Heitz & Neyret) in the shared sampler, so base textures, overlay layers and `n64_textured` props all get it. Sharpened weights → most pixels take 1 sample; overlays check their mask first and skip unpainted pixels; brilinear mip blending (only a band around mip changes reads two levels). Defaults: organic categories on, cement offset-only, patterned (brick, tiles, wood, roof, fabric, metal) off. Measured at 4K internal on an M3 Pro: +0.2 ms for an anti-tiled base, +2.0 ms worst case (4 anti-tiled textures all covering the full screen).
- **N64 3-point filtering** is project-wide (shader global `n64_three_point`, toggled in the Paint panel, saved to project.godot) and applies to all level textures and to `materials/n64_textured.gdshader` — use that shader for props/characters/doors. Bake keeps painted faces unmerged and embeds the mask atlas in the baked materials.
- Entering Paint mode switches the debug grid off.

## Animated textures (scroll & flipbook)

Set on the texture (N64Texture → *Animation* in the inspector), so everything painted with it moves — base textures and overlay layers alike. Runs in the paint shader off one clock, the `n64_time` shader global (GameState sets it each frame; one call). Not `TIME`: a material reading `TIME` makes the Godot editor redraw nonstop. In the editor the clock only runs while *Anim* is on and an animated texture is in use, so still levels keep the editor idle.

- **Scroll**: *Scroll speed* (m/s, negative = backwards) along *Scroll direction*, a world direction projected onto each face — the default (0, −1, 1) runs down walls and towards +Z on floors; a face the direction points straight into stays still. Per-face UV offset / rotation / scale are respected. Waterfalls, rivers, lava, conveyor belts.
- **Flipbook**: *Flip frames*, *Flip columns*, *Flip fps*: frames laid out in a grid on the texture (left to right, top to bottom); one frame covers the tile size. No anti-tiling on flipbooks; filtering never reads across into the next frame. Torch flicker, bubbling, signs.
- **Per layer**: an overlay layer's *Animation → Override scroll* gives it its own speed / direction (the same water flowing at different speeds in different places).
- **Editor**: the toolbar **Anim** toggle (next to AO) freezes them for painting; the game always plays them. Re-bake to ship changes.

## Effects (N64 sprite effects, `addons/n64_runtime/effects/`)

A few flat-colour sprites per effect, simulated in script and drawn as one MultiMesh (no particle system), 3-point filtered and fogged like the levels.

- **N64Effect** (resource, presets in `res://game/effects/`): sprite texture (flipbook: *Frames* in a grid of *Columns*, *Fps*, 0 = once over each sprite's life), *Blend* Mix / Add, *Facing* Camera / Flat (lies across the emit direction: splash rings). Emission: *Burst*, *Rate* (per second: > 0 keeps going), *Max sprites* (oldest recycled), *Radius*. Motion: *Lifetime*, *Speed*, *Spread* (cone around the emit direction), *Gravity* (negative rises), *Drag*, *Spin*. Look: *Size*, *Size end* (× start), *Color* (gradient over life, alpha included). *With*: more effects started together (a splash = droplets + ring).
- **Presets**: dust_puff, water_splash (+ splash_ring), sparkle, torch_fire (flipbook, looping), smoke (looping), hit_flash (+ hit_sparks), clash_spark. Their sprites in `game/effects/sprites/` are generated placeholders — replace the PNGs with real art.
- **One-shot**: `Effects.spawn(effect, position, normal)`; frees itself when done. **Looping**: add an **EffectEmitter** node (emits along its +Y), set *Effect*; it plays in the editor too, *Play* restarts it.
- **Hooked up**: footsteps / landings kick dust on dirt, sand and snow and ripple water (a splash on landing or falling into water); hits flash; blocks and clashes spark.
- **Budget**: 64 sprites per effect at most, 256 across all of them (past it an emitter recycles its oldest sprite).
- **Props**: `n64_textured.gdshader` gained *uv_scroll* (repeats per second) and *flip* (columns, rows, frames, fps) for animated textures on props and doors.

## Surfaces (footsteps & hazards, `addons/n64_runtime/world/surface.gd`)

Every collision face has a **Surface**: a footstep sound (Default, Grass, Dirt, Stone, Wood, Sand, Water, Metal, Snow, Carpet) plus flags — **Slippery**, **Damage**, **Void-out**, **No camera**, and **Climbable** / **Hookshot** (stored for later, nothing reads them yet). The bake works it out per face and splits the collision into one shape per surface (meta `n64_surface` on each CollisionShape3D), so gameplay looks it up with `Surface.of_collision()` / `Surface.of_ray()`.

- **From textures** (painting a texture paints its surface): N64Texture → *Surface* in the inspector. *Auto* picks the sound from the category and name (grass, ground = dirt, brick / rock / cement / tiles = stone, wood, metal, water, fabric = carpet; names with sand, snow, mud, gravel, planks… override), and names with "ice" are slippery. Set a sound or flags to override.
- **Which one a face gets**: its base texture's surface, replaced by the topmost visible overlay layer covering at least half the face (a mud path over grass sounds of mud), then the face's own override.
- **Overrides**: the **Surface** entry at the end of the Paint panel's layer list. Pick a sound (*From texture* keeps the texture's) and flags, then paint with Brush / Paint Face / Fill Face / Fill by Normal / Fill Same Texture; Shift or Erase clears. A painted sound replaces the texture's, flags add to it. While the Surface layer is selected every face is tinted by its surface and the cursor label names the one under it. Re-bake afterwards (changing a texture's surface also needs a re-bake).
- **Gameplay** (every Actor): `floor_surface` holds the ground stood on. Slippery floors make speed build up and die away slowly; Damage floors take a heart and bounce the actor off (once a second); Void-out sends it back to the last safe ground with a heart of damage; No-camera faces sit in their own body on physics layer 2, which actors collide with and the camera arm passes through. Actors with `footsteps` (the player) play `Sfx.footstep()` a stride apart, louder on landing.
- **Sound effects** go through `Sfx.play(name, position)` (`game/audio/sfx.gd`): effect `step_<sound>` for footsteps. A node in group `sfx_player` with `Has(name)` / `Play(name, pitch, volume, pan)` plays them; until the sequenced SFX are hooked up there, footsteps use small generated placeholders.
- **Debug**: **F9** (DebugHud) shows all collision tinted by surface (hue = sound, red damage, black void-out, light blue slippery, fainter no-camera) and the player's current ground.

## Camera zones (`game/world/camera_zone.gd`, `camera_director.gd`)

The player camera is a free orbit until a **CameraZone** takes over (OoT-style camera settings). Add a CameraZone node to a zone scene: it gets a box (orange outline, `size`), an id, and a child Camera3D **View** — select View and tick *Preview* in the 3D toolbar to see the shot.

- **Modes**: *Follow* (dungeon camera: set distance and pitch behind the player; mouse can still turn it unless *Lock turn*, which swings it behind the player), *Fixed* (from View, turning to keep the player in frame if *Track player*), *Parallel* (View's angle, sliding along with the player: corridors, side views), *Frame point* (behind the player facing View's position, keeping both in frame: put View at a boss arena's centre). *Blend time*, *FOV* (0 = keep), *Priority* for overlapping boxes.
- **Painted cameras**: Paint panel → Surface layer → *Camera* lists the scene's CameraZones; painted floor faces switch to that camera while the player stands on them (kept while jumping) and beat every box. A zone with zero size works through paint only. Painted faces show an orange cast in the surface views. Re-bake after painting.
- **Order**: painted floor → highest-priority box containing the player → free orbit. Shots blend over the zone's blend time; going back to the orbit starts it facing the way the view did.
- **Controls**: in Fixed / Parallel / Frame point, movement is relative to the view. After a cut, the held direction keeps its old meaning until the stick is released (so the player doesn't swerve), like OoT.

## Cutscenes (`game/story/cutscene/`, Story workspace → Cutscenes)

Scripted scenes on a timeline, played by the **Cutscenes** autoload: `Cutscenes.play(cutscene)`.

- **Tracks**: *Camera* — shots (cut, move or ease from the previous shot over *Duration*; a transform + FOV, or a CameraZone's View by *Camera id*; *Look at* keeps an actor in frame). One row per **actor** ("player" or a character id; NPCs playing that character, or a node of that name) — Walk / Run to a position (their normal locomotion, so walk cycles match gameplay), Teleport, Face (a direction or another actor), Animate (a model clip, loop or hold; empty = back to normal), Show / Hide. *Dialogue* — a dialogue entry; *Wait* holds the timeline until the box closes. *Events* — Story effects (flags, items, weather…), a sprite effect, a sound, music (whatever is in the `music_player` group gets `play_song`), a screen fade, wait for a button, set the clock.
- **While playing**: clock held, black bars (*Letterbox*), the player and the actors in it are `scripted` (movement from the cues), actors not in it freeze (no AI, no attacks), the camera follows the shots and blends back to gameplay afterwards.
- **Skipping**: hold **Enter / Start**. *Skippable once seen* (default, like OoT), never, or always. A skip still applies every Story event and the clock, and puts actors where the cutscene leaves them (dialogue actions inside skipped lines don't run: put story-critical changes in Story events). Finishing or skipping sets the flag `cutscene/<id>`.
- **Starting one**: a **TriggerVolume** node (box, StoryConditions, *Once* — remembered in `trigger/<zone>/<node>` — then a cutscene, story effects and / or a dialogue), the **Play cutscene** story effect (dialogue actions, schedules; it waits for the conversation to end), or code.
- **Editor** (Story → **Cutscenes**): list + *New* (for the open zone scene), settings row (id, length, skipping, letterbox, zone), a **preview monitor** (the open zone through the cutscene camera at the playhead; actors as coloured ghosts with a facing nose and their route drawn in the scene), the timeline (click the ruler to scrub, ▶ plays it; click a key to edit it in the inspector, drag to move — 0.1 s steps, Shift free — Delete removes), *Add key*, **Camera from view** (a shot from the 3D view, or updates the selected shot), **Position from view** / **Pick in 3D** (click a spot; uses baked collision) / **From selected node** (position + facing of any Node3D), and **▶ Play in game** (runs the zone and plays it; editor runs only). Positions live in the cutscene (zone space): moving the level means re-picking them. Saves by itself; every edit is undoable.
- Example: `game/story/cutscenes/forest_temple_arrival.tres` (fade in on a wide shot, the player walks to the temple stairs on a tracking shot, a cut to the door as it sparkles and a flag is set, then a shot from behind the player).

## Sky & weather (`game/sky/`)

- **DynamicSky** (script on an outdoor zone's WorldEnvironment): N64-style textured sky — zenith → horizon gradient, two drifting layers of an I4 cloud texture thresholded by cloud cover, Majora's Mask style point stars (procedural soft 1–2 N64-pixel dots, pale white / blue / yellow / orange, mostly faint with a few bright, gentle twinkle; no texture, no tiling) that turn with the hour — tune *Star Density / Fill / Size / Twinkle* on the node, sun / moon disc, RGBA16 banding with a 2×2 ordered dither (quantised in sRGB). Also drives linear depth fog (to the horizon colour), flat ambient for actors, and the `n64_weather_tint` shader global that darkens baked level lighting. Leaving the zone resets the tint to white, so interiors without a DynamicSky are unaffected. Editor preview: *Preview Weather* / *Preview Hour* on the node.
- **Weather presets** (`res://game/weather.tres`, Project Settings → **n64/weather/table**): Sunny, Cloudy, Stormy. Each `WeatherPreset` has one `SkyKey` per LightingSchedule key (night, dawn, noon, dusk: zenith, horizon, lit / shadowed cloud colour, stars), so every weather has its own time-of-day variants, blended exactly like the baked lighting. Per preset: cloud cover / softness / speed, world tint (on-screen colour), sun strength, fog begin / end / amount, lightning, and `rain` (0–1, stored for the upcoming rain system).
- **GameState.set_weather(id, seconds)** blends the whole sky / fog / light state between weathers (smoothstep) and is saved with the game; `weather_changed` fires for gameplay. `GameState.weather_now` holds the current blended values. Stormy adds random lightning (strike + flicker every 5–14 s) that flashes the sky, ambient and level tint; `DynamicSky.strike()` triggers one.
- Debug HUD: **F6** cycles weather.
- Sky texture: `res://textures/sky/clouds.res` (I4 64×64), source in `textures/_source/sky/`, rebuildable like any N64Texture.

## N64 Sounds (main screen "N64 Sounds", `addons/n64_audio_tools`)

- **Import CD64 bank…** (toolbar, next to *Import recordings…*): pick a CD64 Audio Unit export folder (or its `<Name>.n64bank.json`). It becomes `res://audio/banks/<name>.res`: samples take the sidecar's ADPCM, books, loops and tuning as they are (never re-encoded), so the bank plays exactly what the plugin previewed; instruments get their zones, envelope, vibrato, volume, pan, program / bank and drum-kit flag. Each sample's original recording is copied to `res://audio/_source/<bank>/<id>.wav` (ignored by Godot, like `textures/_source/`) with its processing settings, so it can be re-processed in the workspace like any recording.
- **Into the open bank**: with another bank open, the import asks whether the export goes into it (next to what it has: a song bank plus a set of effects, say) or into its own bank. Recordings whose id another export already uses in that bank go to `_source/<bank>/<export>/<id>.wav`. Two instruments on the same program are reported (the synth plays the one listed first).
- **Provenance**: everything an import brings is tagged with the export's sidecar `name` (resource metadata `cd64_export`, samples also `cd64_id`; the tree's tooltip names it; stripped from shipping builds). **Re-importing** an export (asks first) touches only what carries its tag, in place: its samples (by id) and instruments (by name) are overwritten — edits to them are lost — and the ones the export dropped are removed (samples with their recordings and every zone playing them). Other exports' and hand-made samples / instruments are left alone. Zones that overlap are reported: the synth plays only the first zone a note falls in.
- **Banks imported before tags** carry none. Re-importing the export a bank was made from (the bank still has the export's name) takes them over: untagged samples whose recordings sit in the bank's import folder (`_source/<bank>/<id>.wav`, which only the importer writes) and untagged instruments named like one in the export that play only those samples get tagged and updated as above. Anything else untagged counts as hand-made and is kept — including an instrument the export has since dropped (delete it by hand). Importing a *different* export into such a bank never takes anything over.
- **Shaping** (sample settings, top of the list): **Start** / **End** (seconds of the recording, End -1 = its end) cut the sample before anything else, so the N64 copy only stores what's heard; **Fade-out** (ms) fades its last part (one-shots only). Drag the orange cut markers on the recording's waveform; the N64 waveform and its loop markers start where the sample does: the cut, plus any leading silence **Trim** removes (loop times count from there, so changing Start or the trim level moves the loop with the sound). CD64 exports carry these settings (`start`, `end`, `fade_ms`), so their samples re-process to the same result.
- **Sample RAM budget**: the toolbar shows the bank's stored samples (ADPCM + books) against **Project Settings → `n64/audio/bank_budget_kb`** (default 512 KB) — yellow past 80 %, red with "⚠ … KB over budget" past it; its tooltip lists each instrument's share (the samples it plays; shared samples count for each) and what no instrument plays. Each instrument's KB is also in the tree.
- **Songs from a CD64 song bank** (one CD64 as the whole synth: channel *n* plays program *n* − 1, the kit on channel 10): a part's instrument list shows the bank's slots first as "ch 3: Strings". Added MIDI parts take their own channel's slot if it's free (else the next free slot); picking a slot moves the part to its channel, and changing a part's channel picks that channel's slot, so each part plays exactly what CD64 previewed. A slot played on another channel shows orange. Song-bank sidecars (`"song": true`) give each instrument its `channel`, kept as instrument metadata `cd64_channel` (1–16) and used for the slots; exports without it count as a song bank when every instrument of one export sits at bank 0, program 0–15, with kits only on program 9.
- Tests: `tests/cd64_make_fixture.gd` builds an export from WAVs with the project's own encoder, `tests/cd64_import_test.gd` checks the round trip, `tests/cd64_provenance_test.gd` several exports + hand-made content in one bank, untagged banks, shaping and the budget numbers, `tests/song_slots_test.gd` the Songs editor with a song bank, `tests/library_test.gd` / `tests/library_ui_test.gd` the library, bank sets and areas, `tests/sfx_test.gd` sound effects (run with `--headless --script`, usage at the top of each).

### Sound effects (`game/audio/N64SfxPlayer.cs`)

- **Sfx bank sets** (Library > a bank set, kind *Sound effects*; a CD64 `"kind": "sfx"` export makes one named after the export) hold the effects: library instruments, played **by name**. In the bank set editor each effect has a **Play** button; the audition bar sets pitch (semitones) and volume (they also bend what's playing), **Stop** stops everything.
- **N64SfxPlayer** (node) plays them on **its own synth**: a separate voice pool (`MaxVoices`, default 8) from the music player's, so effects never steal a music voice — past the limit the quietest / oldest *effect* is stolen. Exports: `Sets` (loaded on ready), `Volume`, `AudioBus`, `Latency`, `MaxInstances` (per effect, 0 = no limit: a new instance stops the oldest of the same effect), `ReverbSend`. Names are looked up across the loaded sets; the first loaded wins a duplicate name (warned).
- API: `Play(name, pitchSemitones = 0, volume = 1, pan = 0, key = -1, maxInstances = -1)` → handle (0 = nothing played, e.g. an unknown name: warned); `key` -1 plays the effect's natural key (first zone's root; kits: the zone's key). `Stop(handle, fadeOut = 0)` (0 = at once, > 0 = fade seconds, < 0 = the instrument's release), `StopAll(fadeOut)`, `IsPlaying`, `SetPitch` / `SetVolume` / `SetPan` on a playing handle, `Load(set)` / `Unload(set)`, `LoadedBytes()`. Positional sound is the caller's job: compute volume / pan from the listener and update them each frame.
- **Areas**: `area.Apply(musicPlayer, sfxPlayer)` loads the area's SFX sets into the effect player (unloading other areas' sets) and gives its first music set to the music player (all its music sets are decoded up front). `N64AudioArea.LoadedBytes(musicPlayer, sfxPlayer)` is the sample RAM held right now (music bank + loaded SFX sets, shared samples once) — compare with `area.WorstCaseBytes()`.

```csharp
var area = GD.Load<N64AudioArea>("res://audio/library/areas/temple.tres");
area.Apply(GetNode<N64MusicPlayer>("Music"), _sfx);   // _sfx: an N64SfxPlayer node
long hum = _sfx.Play("Warp hum", maxInstances: 1);     // looping: plays until stopped
_sfx.SetPitch(hum, speed * 4f);                        // bend it while it plays
_sfx.SetVolume(hum, 1f - distance / 50f); _sfx.SetPan(hum, side);  // positional: caller's job
_sfx.Play("Explosion", pitchSemitones: (float)GD.RandRange(-2.0, 2.0));
_sfx.Stop(hum, 0.3f);                                  // fade out over 0.3 s
```

From GDScript pass every argument (C# defaults don't apply there): `sfx.Play(&"Explosion", 0.0, 1.0, 0.0, -1, -1)`, `sfx.Stop(h, 0.0)`.

## Shipping builds (tooling stays out)

Exports contain only game code and baked data — nothing that shows how levels or textures were authored:

- `addons/n64_level_tools/` is **editor tooling only** and never ships (export presets exclude it; the export plugin skips it too). Runtime pieces live in **`addons/n64_runtime/`** (paint / sampling / prop shaders, LightingSchedule / LightingKey) and `game/`. Game code must not name tool classes (LevelRoot, LevelBrush, BrushShapes, N64Texture, ...): exported builds don't have them. Talk to tooling through duck-typed calls guarded by `Engine.is_editor_hint()`, as Passage does with `sync_floor_brush`.
- The export plugin (`addons/n64_level_tools/export/n64_export_plugin.gd`, registered by the editor plugin):
  - replaces every **LevelRoot** in exported scenes with plain built-in nodes built from its bake (Node3D → `Mesh` MeshInstance3D + `Collision` StaticBody3D): no script, brushes, paint masks, vertex edits or lighting keys. Node exports pointing at the level (e.g. `Passage.level`) are re-pointed. Unbaked levels are an export error, stale bakes a warning — **Bake All before exporting**;
  - converts **N64Textures** to plain ImageTextures (drops source paths and import settings);
  - skips **LevelData** files.
- Presets (`export_presets.cfg`: macOS, Windows Desktop) also exclude `screenshots/`, `*.md`, `build/`, `textures/_source/`, and ship scripts as **compressed binary tokens** (no GDScript source text). Shaders necessarily ship as shader source.
- Binary tokens deter casual reading, not determined reverse engineering. For more, enable **PCK encryption** (Export → Encryption), which needs export templates compiled with your `SCRIPT_AES256_ENCRYPTION_KEY`; a determined attacker can still extract the key from the binary.
- Verify a build: `--export-pack "macOS" build/test/m3d3.zip`, then `unzip -l` it — no `n64_level_tools`, `*_level.tres` or `.gd` files should be listed; run it with `--main-pack build/test/m3d3.zip`.

## World structure (`game/`)

```
/root
├── GameState      autoload: clock, day, flags, save/load        (game/game_state.gd)
├── ZoneManager    autoload: travel, loading screen, debug HUD    (game/zone_manager.gd)
│   └── Player     persists across zones                          (player/player.tscn)
└── <Zone scene>   current zone, swapped by ZoneManager.travel()
    ├── Room ─ LevelRoot geometry + "Actors" (respawned on each entry)
    ├── RoomPortal (between two rooms, kept outside rooms)
    ├── Passage    (OoT corridor out of the zone: floor + fade to black + waypoint)
    ├── Door       (OoT door: joins two rooms, or leads to another zone)
    ├── ZoneExit   (bare waypoint trigger → other zone + spawn id)
    └── SpawnPoint (spawn id + optional room)
```

- **Zone links check** (pre-build): every ZoneExit, zone Door, Passage node and passage brush must name a target zone that exists (errors), and should ask for a spawn that zone has (warnings: the game falls back to its default spawn). Runs at the start of every export (errors in the Output panel and the export log; turn off with Project Settings > `n64/export/check_zone_links`) and on demand from **Project → Tools → M3D World → Check Zone Links…**. Never while editing, so links to zones that don't exist yet are fine during development. (`addons/n64_level_tools/zone_links.gd`.)
- **New zones**: **Project → Tools → M3D World → New Outdoor Zone… / New Indoor Zone…** — pick a .tscn path; creates the scene (Zone root, LevelRoot with a floor grid and its LevelData saved as `<name>_level.tres`, `default` SpawnPoint) and opens it. Outdoor adds a DynamicSky WorldEnvironment and a DayNightSun named `Sun` (clock runs); indoor adds a dark ambient environment and a ceiling OmniLight, with `time_flows` off. Levels start in Dynamic lighting — switch to Baked Vertex / Baked Timed and Bake when ready. Link zones with Passages / Doors / ZoneExits (`target_zone` + `target_spawn`).
- **Zone** — root node of a zone scene. `time_flows` pauses the clock (dungeons). Any zone scene can be run directly (F6).
- **Room** — removed from the scene tree when not needed. States: unloaded / geometry (visible through a doorway) / active (geometry + fresh copy of its `Actors` child). Actors reset on re-entry; use flags for permanent changes.
- **RoomPortal** — trigger in a doorway. Inside it, both rooms are active; leaving picks the room on that side (arrow = `room_b`). `preload_neighbors` keeps adjacent room geometry loaded.
- **Passage** — placed with the Passage tool (last button in the shape grid): click a wall (opens outward through it, at the floor height below) or a floor (opens the way the camera faces). A passage is **one level brush** (Object mode: select, move, delete like any brush) carrying a `PassageSpec` (inspector: *Passage* — waypoint `target_zone` / `target_spawn` / `spawn_id`, width / height / depth, fade, dressing). The level builds from it: the **corridor floor** (~1 m cells, the clicked floor's texture with its UV scale / rotation / offset turned and shifted so the pattern carries straight on across the threshold), through a wall the **side walls + ceiling** (the wall's texture), and its **Passage node** (ZoneExit deep in the corridor at `trigger_start`, DarknessVolume, arrival spawn `spawn_id` just inside) as a generated child of the LevelRoot — never saved with the scene, rebuilt from the brush in the editor and in game, and written out as a real node on export so zones still find the spawn. Placed on a wall, it also **carves the opening** (width × height, from the floor up) through every wall surface parallel to it up to 2 m deep — edge loops are cut first, so paint around it stays. **Deleting** the brush (Delete, or **Cmd+click** it with the Passage tool) closes the wall opening again from its rim — one undo step. The **resize squares** set its width (either side), height (top) and depth (far end); the corridor, fade and trim follow, and the **wall opening is cut again** to match whenever the passage's size or place changes (squares and moves: in the same undo step; inspector edits: a step of their own). The opening carved is remembered on the PassageSpec (`carved_*`). Placing or removing re-bakes vertex AO when the level uses it. The fade begins `fade_lead` (1.5 m) in front of the opening and reaches black at `fade_end` (0.9 of the depth). Older Passage nodes (with their own floor / shell brushes) keep working and are removed the old way.
- **Door** — Door tool: click a wall. A door is **one level brush** (Object mode: select, move, resize, delete like any brush) carrying a `DoorSpec` (inspector: *Door* — mode, style, rooms / zone, lock, `door_id`, size, panel surface / picture, wall faces, dressing; *Floor* / *Walls* lining textures above it). The level builds from it: on a solid wall the **opening is cut** through it and **lined** (sides + top in the wall's texture, a floor carrying on the floor's pattern), the **trim** (dressing) is built in (baked like the walls), and invisible pick faces sit where the panel hangs; the **Door node** (panel, interaction, rooms / travel, lock) is generated under the Zone, outside any Room (so it never unloads with one) — never saved with the scene, rebuilt from the brush in the editor and in game, written out as a real node on export. Rooms are paths from the scene's root (`room_a` on -Z, `room_b` on +Z / the arrow; zone doors: `arrival_room`), set by the tool from the Rooms on either side. Resizing (squares or inspector) or moving cuts the opening again; deleting (Delete, or **Cmd+click** with the Door tool) closes it and removes the next room's wall / starter floor it built while untouched. On a single-sided wall (nothing behind) nothing is cut and the door sits on its face. Recess insets make the lining a tunnel. Older Door **nodes** keep working as before (their own *Panel surface* slot, re-cut on resize). Between two rooms it joins them (ROOM mode); otherwise it leads to another zone (ZONE mode). Press `interact` (E / pad A) nearby: control locks, the door opens (wooden swing or dungeon slide shutter), the player walks through, it closes, and rooms load/unload — the room behind a closed door stays unloaded. ZONE doors open onto black and travel; arrivals (`spawn_id`) step out and the door closes behind them. Locks: flag, or small key (per-zone count `<zone>/small_keys`, consumed once, door stays unlocked).
- **Arched openings** (Door and Passage → *Shape* → *Top*: **Flat**, **Round** or **Pointed**; *Arch rise* = how much of the height the arch takes, 0 = half the width round / an equilateral pointed arch): the wall is cut along the arch (and the trim's outer arch) segment by segment, through only the faces each segment crosses, so no lines run across the wall; the lining's ceiling follows the arch, the trim becomes posts up to the springing plus a ring of voussoirs *Top height* thick (*Top depth* proud), the door panel is arched (with matching collision) and a passage's fade follows the arch. Changing the shape cuts the wall again. (`game/world/opening_shape.gd`.)
- **Dressing** (Door and Passage → *Dressing* slot): an **OpeningDressing** resource — casing / trim around the opening: left post, right post and top bar, each its own faces (front + extruded edges), one *texture* for fronts and edges at the level's texel density (the texture's world size, like the walls; *Texture scale* makes it bigger / smaller). Built-in trim and arched linings get exact UVs: fronts line up with the wall, edges and arch soffits are unwrapped at true size (no stretching on slanted faces). *Joint*: **Butt** (top bar over the posts, horizontal seams) or **Mitre** (45° diagonal seams). *Side width* / *Top height* = how far the trim reaches out from the opening; *Side depth* / *Top depth* = how far posts and bar stand out from the wall (a deeper top bar gives the OoT lintel look). Doors dress *Both* / *Front* / *Back* wall faces (the Door tool records the wall thickness as `wall_depth` / `wall_center_z`); passages dress their room side, **built into the level** with the corridor (passage brushes: lit, baked, AO'd and textured exactly like the walls; the texture joins the level palette). Save the resource as a .tres to reuse a trim on many openings. On doors (and older Passage nodes) it's generated at runtime (game code, ships), with collision, lit in real time like the door panel; pieces are sliced every ~0.5 m so shading has vertices. **AO:** the trim casts occlusion in every bake (vertex AO and lighting) and receives its own AO from *Bake AO* (stored on the Door / Passage, applied through vertex alpha, hidden by the editor AO toggle, cleared by *Clear*). Changing the trim's shape drops its AO until the next Bake AO.
- **DarknessVolume** — per-pixel fade to black along local -Z for every opaque mesh inside it (level, props, player). Past its far end shows black. Usable on its own.
- **ZoneExit** — fades out, threaded-loads `target_zone`, places the player at `target_spawn`.
- **GameState** — `time_of_day`, `day`, `get_day_name()`, `set_flag(id, value, temporary)`, `get_flag(id)`; temp flags clear on zone change. `zone.flag("x")` scopes ids as `zone_id/x`. Saves JSON to `user://save_<slot>.json`.
- **Debug HUD** (in game) — F3 toggle, hold T to fast-forward time, F2 save, F4 load.

Play controls: WASD or arrow keys move, Space jumps, mouse looks. Debug: F3 HUD, hold T fast time, F2 / F4 save / load, F6 weather, **F7 profiler**, F11 fullscreen. The profiler (top right, updates 4×/s) shows FPS and frame time (worst of the last second), CPU process / physics time, triangles and draw calls this frame (coloured against budgets in `game/ui/debug_hud.gd`: `BUDGET_TRIANGLES` 6000, `BUDGET_DRAW_CALLS` 150 — yellow past 80 %, red past 100 %), objects, VRAM / texture / buffer memory, RAM, node count and physics bodies / pairs. Triangle and draw counts include shadow passes. The game window starts maximized at native resolution (Project Settings → Display → Window).

Demo: `zones/overworld` (day/night sun, passage to the temple, back door into the Vault) and `zones/temple` (Entrance → shutter → Hall (small key) → locked shutter → Vault (gem, door out to the overworld)).
